# novelty

**KL surprise scores for U.S. trademark goods/services descriptions across the
full USPTO backfile, joined to SEC EDGAR financials.** We measure each filing's
*vocabulary flux* — how much its goods/services prose has moved with or against
where its NICE-class language was heading — and ask how flux predicts
post-registration survival, financial outcomes, and IPO rates.

The paper, with all figures and regressions, lives in
[`paper/main.pdf`](paper/main.pdf). The headline finding is that
post-registration 5-year survival is U-shaped in ΔKL: filings whose
vocabulary is *moving with* the field's direction of travel and filings
whose vocabulary is *moving against* it both outperform filings whose
vocabulary sits flux-neutral in the middle. The squeeze on flux-neutral
filings is the most robust pattern in the corpus.

## What the paper claims

1. **Flux is not distinctiveness.** The two component surprises ($KL_\text{pros}$
   against the recent past, $KL_\text{retr}$ against the near future) are nearly
   collinear at the filing level ($\rho \approx 0.97$). Their *level* is general
   distinctiveness; their *difference* $\Delta KL$ is vocabulary flux — the only
   directional component.
2. **The flux-neutral middle dies.** Post-registration 5-year survival is U-shaped
   in $\Delta KL$ under every outcome operationalisation: examiner approval,
   reach-and-still-live at 5y, and the SEC-listed firm proxy. The dip sits at
   $\Delta KL \approx 0$ and the lift at both extremes is 5–10 percentage points.
3. **Both extractive strategies work.** Negative-$\Delta KL$ filings — those
   harvesting sunsetting vocabulary like typesetting, dial-up, e-liquid, pre-craft
   beer — survive at 50–80 %. Positive-$\Delta KL$ filings — those catching
   the cannabis, crypto, vape, NFT and AI waves — survive at 50–82 %. Tokens
   in the flux-neutral middle (`organisational`, `conformity`, `freighting`) survive
   at 11–26 %.
4. **Novelty of language and novelty of claim decouple.** Amazon was the
   174th firm to claim "online retail" but firm-mean $\Delta KL \approx 0$; Uber,
   Lyft, and Airbnb never used their category buzzword at all but had first-filing
   $\Delta KL$ values in the $+0.7$ range. Both kinds of novelty raise survival;
   the substantive findings come from their interaction.
5. **Mechanism is not separable.** Whether the survival lift at negative $\Delta KL$
   reflects intelligent harvesting, passive late-occupation of a legacy category,
   or selection on something unobservable cannot be teased apart with the trademark
   record alone. The empirical pattern — squeeze on the centre, lift at both
   extremes — is unambiguous; its mechanism is not.

## How to build the paper

```bash
# end-to-end (requires Python 3.11 and ~20GB free for the TM backfile)
make setup tm sec crosswalk analysis paper

# or simply
make all
```

`make help` lists the individual targets. The build is incremental: editing one
analysis script and re-running `make paper` rebuilds only that figure and
re-compiles the LaTeX. `make clean-results` forces a full figure rebuild.

The TM backfile download is heavy (`make tm` takes hours and writes
~15 GB to `data/processed/`). Once the per-class Parquet panels and the
SEC crosswalk exist, `make analysis paper` regenerates everything from
them in well under an hour and produces `paper/main.pdf`.

Requires Python 3.11, a TeX install (TeX Live), and a free USPTO Open Data
Portal API key from <https://data.uspto.gov> (My ODP → My API Key); set it
via `cp .env.example .env` and edit `USPTO_ODP_API_KEY=...`.

## Stage timing on a fresh checkout

| Stage | Wall-clock | Output |
|---|---|---|
| `setup` | < 5 min | `.venv/` |
| `tm` | hours | `data/processed/{tm,vocab,surprise,firm_year,outcomes}_class<NNN>.parquet` × 45 |
| `sec` | minutes | `data/processed/sec_firm_year.parquet` |
| `crosswalk` | minutes | `data/processed/uspto_sec_crosswalk.parquet` |
| `analysis` | ~ 1 hour | `paper/results/*.{png,csv,tex,txt,json}` |
| `paper` | < 1 min | `paper/main.pdf` |

## Adding a new analysis

Drop a script into `scripts/`, write its output into `paper/results/`, add a
target for it in the Makefile (one line for the script invocation, plus an
entry in the `FIGURES` list so `make paper` notices it), and `\input` /
`\includegraphics` the result from `paper/main.tex`.

## Data not in this repo

The raw USPTO backfile (~12 GB), per-class records/vocab/surprise/outcome
Parquets (~15 GB), and SEC EDGAR FSDS ZIPs (~7 GB) are excluded by
`.gitignore`. The reproduction pipeline regenerates them from public sources.
