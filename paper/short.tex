\title{Vocabulary flux predicts trademark survival, but not in the

\large A short report on a $10$-million-filing $\Delta KL$ analysis}
\author{Eric Silver}
\affil{}
\date{May 2026}

\begin{abstract}
We score every U.S.\ trademark filing in $1990$--$2021$ for the
\emph{flux} of its goods/services vocabulary --- whether the language
the firm picked anticipates where its industry was going or extracts
from where the industry has been --- using a Kullback--Leibler
surprise difference $\Delta KL = KL_{\text{pros}} - KL_{\text{retr}}$
against per-day exponentially decay-weighted reference distributions
of the same NICE class. On $10{,}060{,}073$ clean filings,
post-registration $5$-year survival is \emph{U-shaped} in $\Delta KL$:
filings with strongly forward-flux vocabulary and filings with strongly
backward-flux vocabulary both survive at $50$--$55\%$; the
flux-neutral middle survives at $\sim 45\%$. The U holds in $27$ of
$44$ NICE classes, in $23$ of $32$ individual filing-year cohorts,
and within firms that demonstrably persisted past at least one
Section-8 lapse. We rule out nine alternative explanations: the
shape is not an examination-pipeline artefact, a vintage effect, a
foreign-extension artefact, an English-language-drift artefact, or a
firm-level survival signal in disguise. The shape is mediated by
local vocabulary-cluster viability: $84\%$ of the $\Delta KL$
quadratic on survival is absorbed by counting how many of each
filing's near-competitors also survived their own 5-year window. We
present the headline U, the rejection of nine alternatives, and the
viability mediator. A long companion paper
(\texttt{main.pdf}) reports the per-industry detail, the financial
outcomes, the firm-level returns, and the per-NICE-class deep dives.
\end{abstract}

\section{The headline finding}

Figure~\ref{fig:short-headline} is the entire paper in two panels. Each
panel pools the $10$M clean filings across all $44$ NICE classes;
each curve bins filings by quantile of one $KL$ measure and plots the
mean post-registration $5$-year survival rate (left) or examiner
approval rate alone (right) in the bin. Blue is $\Delta KL$ (the
flux signal); red is prospective $KL$ (how unusual the filing's
vocabulary is against the recent past); green is retrospective $KL$

\paragraph{Reading the U.} The flux-neutral middle of the $\Delta KL$
distribution --- filings whose vocabulary is equally close to past
and future, sitting on the modal language of the modal moment --- has
the lowest post-registration survival rate in the corpus. Both
extremes outperform it: filings whose vocabulary moved \emph{with}
where the field was going (positive $\Delta KL$, the cannabis,
crypto, NFT, vape, and AI waves) and filings whose vocabulary
extracted \emph{from} where the field had been (negative $\Delta KL$,
microfiche, dial-up, pager, pre-craft beer, pre-fintech insurance
boilerplate). The depth of the dip is $5$--$10$ percentage points
across operationalisations; the curve looks like a smile, not a
frown.

\paragraph{This is surprising.} It is the opposite shape from what
the optimal-distinctiveness literature (Deephouse 1999, \emph{SMJ};
Zhao et al.\ 2017, \emph{SMJ}) predicts. It is larger in magnitude
than any single mechanism we can articulate explains. And it has
survived every alternative-explanation test we have run.

\section{Method in one paragraph}

For each filing $i$ at filing-date $t_i$ in NICE class $c$, the
goods/services term distribution $P_i$ is compared via support-restricted
Kullback--Leibler divergence to two reference distributions
$Q_i^{\text{pros}}$ and $Q_i^{\text{retr}}$ built by per-day exponential
decay-weighting (half-life $H = 2$ years; chosen by sweep on Class~9
to maximise corr$(\Delta KL, \text{survived\_5y})$ over the
range $H \in \{0.5, 1, 2, 3, 5\}$) of all other filings in $c$ before
and after $t_i$ respectively. $KL_i^{\text{pros}}$ is in nats; a typical
filing scores $5$ nats on each side ($e^5 \approx 150\times$
per-token likelihood ratio against the reference); their difference
$\Delta KL_i$ is the directional residual after the common distinctiveness
factor is removed and has a much tighter range, $[-0.30, +0.34]$ nats
at the $5$th and $95$th percentiles. The two components are highly
correlated at the per-filing level ($\rho = +0.99$), so the
\emph{level} of either is approximately the same number; the
\emph{difference} carries the directional signal. The clean analytic
panel is the $10{,}060{,}073$ filings in $1990$--$2021$ with Kish
$N_{\text{eff}} \geq 1{,}000$ on both reference windows and at least
three vocabulary tokens. Outcomes are derived from USPTO status
codes: $\texttt{survived\_5y} = \texttt{reached\_registration}$ AND
$\texttt{currently\_live}$ at year 5 after filing.

\paragraph{What we learned from the mediator.} The decisive
falsification is (10), the competition-density mediation. We took
$10{,}000$ stratified Class-9 (Software \& Electronics) focal filings,
vectorised the full Class-9 corpus with TF-IDF, and for each focal
counted near-competitors (cosine $\geq 0.7$) in the window
$[t_F - 2y,\, t_F + 5y]$ along with how many of those neighbours
survived their own 5-year window. The result is in
Figure~\ref{fig:short-mediation}.

\begin{figure}[h]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=\linewidth]{results/competition_density.png}
\caption{\textbf{The U on survival is mediated by neighbour
viability.} Per $\Delta KL$ decile on $10{,}000$ Class-9 focal
filings, left panel: count of total and live (survived-5y) near-competitors
within $[t-2y,\, t+5y]$. The flux-neutral middle has the fewest
live peers; both flux extremes are in clusters with $\sim 2$--$3\times$

(see companion paper Appendix~C) shows
$\log(1 + \text{live\_competitors})$ absorbs $84\%$ of the $\Delta KL$
quadratic, but the linear $z(\Delta KL)$ effect on survival is
preserved.}
\label{fig:short-mediation}

The substantive reframe: flux extremes do not signal absence of
competition --- they signal \emph{viable} clusters where peers can
also operate. The flux-neutral middle is where firms are choosing
vocabularies that the next 5 years' filings will also abandon. The
U-shape on survival is then a niche-viability signal mediated through
$\Delta KL$; flux is partly a marker of which corner of the
vocabulary supports real businesses. After the mediator is removed,
the residual flux effect on survival is monotone-positive: forward
flux still helps independently, just less than the bare bivariate
coefficient claims.

\section{What we know and what we don't}

\paragraph{What we know.} (i) Post-registration 5-year survival is
U-shaped in $\Delta KL$ across $44$ NICE classes, $32$ filing-year
cohorts, and at the token level (both negative-stratum tokens like
\texttt{microfiche, dial-up, pager} and positive-stratum tokens like
\texttt{blockchain, cannabidiol, non-fungible} survive at well above
the corpus baseline; flux-neutral tokens like \texttt{organisational,
conformity, freighting} survive at $11$--$26\%$ vs.\ a $\sim 49\%$
corpus baseline). (ii) The U is a cluster-viability signal mediated
through $\Delta KL$; $84\%$ of the quadratic dissolves under the
competition-density control. (iii) A linear $z(\Delta KL)$ effect on
survival remains after the mediator is controlled --- forward flux
helps independently. (iv) The signal is filing-level, not just
firm-level: within firms that abandoned some marks and kept others
alive, the U is preserved.

\paragraph{What we don't know.} (i) Whether the negative-flux tail
reflects intelligent harvesting (firms recognising a category in
decline and operating from it with their eyes open) or passive
late-occupation (firms whose business naturally uses the receding
vocabulary because they are still doing the legacy thing) or
selection on something unobservable (firms surviving long enough to
file legacy-vocabulary trademarks have already proved durable). The
trademark record does not distinguish these. (ii) Why the linear
$z(\Delta KL)$ effect survives the cluster-viability control. The
most plausible explanation is that conditional on a viable cluster,
firms whose vocabulary moves \emph{toward} the cluster's future are
ahead of the firms whose vocabulary moves \emph{against} it ---
within-cluster directional information that competition-density
alone misses. (iii) Whether the firm-level financial reward to flux
($+3.2$ pp gross margin per $\sigma$ of trailing $\Delta KL$ for
public firms) is robust to the cluster-viability control. We have
not yet computed the firm-level mediation regression.

\paragraph{What this is not.} This is not a paper about
distinctiveness in the optimal-distinctiveness sense. The two
component $KL$s --- prospective and retrospective surprise on their
own --- measure distinctiveness, and on the level of distinctiveness
there is no clean inverted-U. The U on $\Delta KL$ is the U on the
\emph{difference} between the two, which is by construction the
orthogonal direction in PCA terms (PC1 $\approx$ distinctiveness,
PC2 $\approx \Delta KL$ = flux). Flux is not distinctiveness; the
two measure orthogonal things and the empirical regularities on each
are different.
