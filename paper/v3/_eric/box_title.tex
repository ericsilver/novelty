\textbf{My recommendation: \#12}, amended to current terminology ---
\emph{Business Themes in the Trademark Record: Language Signals of Product
Survival, Funding, and Listing}. RP titles are declarative and put the data
and indicator first; \#12 is the only candidate built that way, it names the
record (RP's readers care what corpus this is), and it survives truncation
to its pre-colon half. \textbf{Runner-up: \#9}, which is the plainest
statement of the construct; its cost is that ``vocabulary position'' is our
coinage and means nothing to a reader browsing a table of contents.
\#1 and \#11 carry the paper's voice best but read as management-journal or
trade-press titles at RP; \#7 makes a claim (``fails the use test'') that a
referee will treat as the conclusion asserted in the title. If you want the
arrows sentence, it is already the first line of the introduction, which is
where RP papers keep their one flourish.
