# Twelve candidate titles (2026-08-23)

Brief: linguistic tells and business themes predict success, survival, and thriving.

1. Arrows in Their Backs: Early Language and the Survival of Trademarked Products
2. Linguistic Tells of Commercial Risk: What Trademark Descriptions Predict About Survival and Success
3. Written Too Early: Business Themes, Trademark Language, and the Five-Year Test of Use
4. The Words Before the Product: Theme-Level Surprise in Trademark Filings and What Follows It
5. Early, Unusual, or Ordinary: Trademark Vocabulary and the Life of a Product
6. Pioneers and Their Words: Predicting Trademark Survival and Firm Success from Goods-and-Services Language
7. Leading Language Fails the Use Test: Business Themes as Tells in 14 Million Trademark Filings
8. What a Trademark Says Before the Market Does: Theme Surprise, Survival, and Thriving
9. Speaking Ahead of the Industry: Vocabulary Position in Trademark Filings and the Outcomes That Follow
10. Themes, Tells, and Gates: Trademark Language as a Predictor of Product Survival and Firm Success
11. The Cost of Being Early: Trademark Vocabulary, Use-Proof Survival, and Who Thrives
12. Business Themes in the Trademark Record: Linguistic Signals of Survival, Funding, and Listing

Notes for choosing. 1, 6 and 11 carry the introduction's frame. 2, 7 and 10 name the
method ("tells", "themes") in the title, which helps search but reads as jargon to a
trademark audience. 3, 5 and 9 are the plainest. 8 and 12 put the record first, which
suits a data-paper framing and Research Policy's conventions. The subtitle in every case
is doing the work of saying "predict"; if a journal wants a short title, the part before
the colon stands alone for 1, 3, 5, 8 and 11.
