\section{Robustness: what changes under other choices}\label{sec:robust}

Each result above is stated under one formulation chosen for legibility:
one theme resolution, flat weighting inside the reference windows,
symmetric five-year windows, estimates conditional on grant, and the
2002--2018 registration cohorts. The table summarizes what each choice
costs; the paragraphs carry the four checks a reader will probe, and the
supplementary material carries the full treatment of everything else ---
functional forms, the term-scored replication and its firm-level hazard,
the listing-versus-reporting split, the window-mix decomposition, and the
anatomy of $L$ as a small residual of two large levels. No sign anywhere
depends on any of these choices.

\emph{Representation and weighting.} Four per-filing builds exist: themes
or terms, flat or two-year-half-life weighting. Weighting is close to
irrelevant (lead correlates at $0.97$--$0.98$ across it, atypicality at
$1.000$); representation is the real test (themes and terms agree at only
$0.46$ on lead), and it is the conservative one --- term scoring returns
larger lead contrasts ($+3.56$ and $+2.92$pp) and smaller atypicality
contrasts ($-3.63$, $-3.73$) than the production build's $+2.29$ and
$-4.39$.

\emph{Resolution and fitting noise.} Re-fitting the theme model at
$T \in \{50, 100, 200, 500\}$ leaves the sign and significance of the
software-class contrast untouched while the magnitude moves
non-monotonically between $+2.7$ and $+5.7$pp; corpus-wide, the only two
resolutions that exist agree at $+2.29$ and $+2.23$pp. A second fit at
$T = 200$ differing only in its random seed agrees with the first at
$r = 0.79$ on lead --- most of the apparent resolution sensitivity is
fitting noise, the estimate moves only $+5.20 \to +4.88$pp, and the
implied measurement error attenuates every lead coefficient toward zero
rather than manufacturing it.

\emph{Decayed windows.} Weighting recent language up with a two-year
half-life is the one choice that shifts the headline: $+1.95$pp against
$+2.29$, the same sign, precision, and profile. The flat window is kept
as the simpler statement; a reader who prefers recency should carry
$+1.95$.

\emph{Conditioning and composition.}\label{sec:robust_uncond} Without the
grant filter, the composite outcome (registered and passed the proof) is
nearly flat in lead because two opposed gradients cancel --- registration
rising, survival falling --- so conditioning exposes the market's
gradient rather than creating it. Excluding the 47\% of registrations
whose exact normalized text is shared with another filing
\emph{strengthens} both headline gradients (lead $+3.05$pp, atypicality
$-5.68$); identical texts carry identical scores and cluster at the
typical end, diluting both. The non-monotone profiles on signed lead
decompose into a symmetric part inherited from atypicality composition, a
symmetric conditional lead-magnitude effect, and a small linear tilt
whose fitted vertices land where that arithmetic puts them (supplementary
material).

Two cautions carry across every raw contrast: outcomes correlate within
owner at $0.38$, so unclustered $t$-statistics in the per-class, per-era,
and per-episode cells overstate precision by roughly a factor of two; and
the penalty is not constant over the period, so the pooled row is an
average over the swing of Section~\ref{sec:eras}, and a reader who wants
the penalty for a period should read Figure~\ref{fig:cohortslopes}
rather than the pooled estimate.
